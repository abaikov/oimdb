OIMDB — THE PRICE OF EVERY OPERATION AGAINST THE PRIMITIVE IT REPLACES

Each row is one call this library offers, timed against the plain JavaScript a person would have
written instead of it. Not against another library — against the floor. The ratio is ours over
the floor, and it is a price tag rather than a ranking.

WHERE THE FLOOR AND OUR CALL ARE NOT DOING THE SAME WORK

There is no pair in this suite whose two sides do identical work, and a floor that does less is a
lie in our favour. Two of these are structural and apply to most rows: a Map-keyed floor pays a
hash where our identity path does not, which flatters us; and our write additionally marks a channel
and may route to indexes the floor knows nothing about, which does not. Each row says its own.

A THIRD ONE BELONGS TO THE INSTRUMENT AND APPLIES TO EVERY ROW. Both sides are driven by a loop —
an increment, a bounds check, an index wrap and one store into a sink — and that loop is charged
to the numerator and the denominator alike. Adding the same constant to both pulls a ratio toward
1.00, and it pulls hardest where the operation is cheapest, so the sub-nanosecond rows are the ones
this understates most. The wrap is a mask rather than a division for exactly that reason. Read
`find.vs-map-get` as the calibration: it is one call and one Map.get on our side against one Map.get
on the floor, so whatever it reports above 1.00 is mostly this.

flush.empty-vs-noop   [per frame]   30.1x
  ours   db.flush() with nothing marked and nobody subscribed
  floor  a call that bumps a frame counter
  THE FLOOR HERE IS ESSENTIALLY ZERO AND THE RATIO IS THEREFORE NOT A READING — take the
  absolute nanoseconds on our side and ignore the multiple. A hand-written store has no frame
  to close, so the nearest thing to a floor is the bookkeeping a person writes instead, and V8
  inlines that to almost nothing. What ours does for the money: runs the effect graph's drain,
  swaps the two delivery lists, walks an empty delivery, runs the after-flush and
  after-each-frame phases, sweeps identities nobody holds and reports errors. This number is
  the fixed cost every other frame row carries, which is what makes it worth printing even
  though its ratio means nothing.
  measured  1,317,572 ops/pass ours, 44,067,074 floor; 9 samples; warm-up 21 planned, 21/21 reached; spread 16.8%/8.3%; load 2.73 → 2.73 on 8 cores

subscribeToField.deliver-vs-guarded-call   [per frame]   27.0x
  ours   db.subscribeToField(slot, 'title', h) — the narrowest signal the engine has
  floor  store the field and call h only when the name that moved is 'title'
  THE ONE COLUMN THAT MOVED IS THE ONE SUBSCRIBED TO, on both sides, so this is the happy
  case. Ours pays for it twice over: the write now RECORDS which columns moved (a per-identity
  list it would not otherwise keep — see the `movedFields` machinery), and the handler
  consults that list on delivery. The floor tests a string it already has. NOT PRICED HERE,
  and worth knowing before quoting this row: a field subscription is an ordinary subscription
  that filters, so two field subscriptions on one identity both wake and one returns having
  found its name absent — a write to `note` costs a `title` subscriber a frame and a delivery
  to discover it had nothing to show, where the floor's guard would not have been reached at
  all.
  measured  385,038 ops/pass ours, 11,152,792 floor; 9 samples; warm-up 20 planned, 20/20 reached; spread 7.3%/10.2%; load 2.54 → 2.66 on 8 cores

subscribe.deliver-vs-direct-call   [per frame]   25.3x
  ours   update the row, flush, and the subscription fires — per identity delivered
  floor  store the field and call the handler — per row
  ONE WRITE PER IDENTITY PER FRAME, WHICH IS THE WORST CASE FOR US. Both sides call the
  handler exactly once per row, so nothing here is saved by coalescing — the mechanism ours
  pays for on every write is deliberately given nothing to do. Ours per operation: the whole
  update path, then the stamp, then a push onto the frame's pending list, then the frame
  walking that list and asking the subscriber store to deliver. The floor: one store and one
  call. The fixed cost of the frame itself is divided by a thousand deliveries here;
  `flush.empty-vs-noop` prices it directly so a reader can subtract it. What ours buys and the
  floor cannot: the handler is registered against the identity, so a writer does not have to
  know who is listening.
  measured  684,128 ops/pass ours, 18,296,832 floor; 9 samples; warm-up 20 planned, 20/20 reached; spread 7.0%/10.8%; load 2.67 → 2.67 on 8 cores

computed.recompute-vs-hand-memo   [per frame]   24.9x
  ours   db.computed over one row, invalidated by a write and recomputed at the frame
  floor  set a dirty flag on write, recompute on read if dirty
  THE FLOOR IS A REAL MEMO AND DOES THE SAME JOB: it is invalidated by the write, it
  recomputes once however many writes landed, and it answers the same number. Two things it
  does not do. It recomputes LAZILY, at the read, where ours recomputes eagerly during the
  frame — so a value nobody reads is free for the floor and paid for by us. And it has no
  graph: a memo over another memo has to be invalidated by hand and in the right order, which
  is the whole of what `db.computed` is buying and is not exercised by a one-level derivation.
  Direction: inflates our ratio, and a deeper graph would move it back the other way by an
  amount this row cannot show. Ours additionally gates on equality before marking anything
  downstream, and the derivation here always changes, so that gate never saves us a wake-up.
  measured  592,264 ops/pass ours, 14,279,128 floor; 9 samples; warm-up 20 planned, 20/20 reached; spread 15.4%/5.6%; load 2.66 → 2.66 on 8 cores

THE CONTROLS, IN FULL

control-resolution-2x   1.94x
  ours   two units of arithmetic per iteration
  floor  one unit of arithmetic per iteration
  NOT A PRICE — an instrument check, and it is printed first because nothing below it means
  anything until it passes. The true ratio is a little UNDER 2: both sides pay the driving
  loop once, so the exact expectation is (2u + L) / (u + L) with L the loop and u one unit,
  which at sixteen dependent steps per unit puts it around 1.9. A reading below 1.5 means this
  process could not resolve a deliberate doubling, and the runner voids the report rather than
  printing prices under it.

control-scale-same-body   1.01x
  ours   one unit of arithmetic per iteration
  floor  the same body again, over its own copy of the same state
  NOT A PRICE — the noise floor. The two sides are identical code over identical,
  separately-allocated state, so the true ratio is 1.00 exactly and every departure from it
  printed here was produced by this machine at this moment. The rule that follows is the one
  the whole table is read under: NO RATIO CLOSER TO 1.00 THAN THIS GAP IS A READING. It also
  bounds the per-row noise figure, which is the same idea measured again inside every pair —
  each pair times its floor twice, once before our side and once after, and prints how far the
  two disagreed.
