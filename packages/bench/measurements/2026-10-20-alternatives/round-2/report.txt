THE INSTRUMENT, BEFORE ANY VERDICT

ratio   ours  floor  noise  expected  control
1.95x  38 ns  19 ns   1.4%     ~1.9x  control-resolution-2x
1.00x  19 ns  20 ns   1.0%     1.00x  control-scale-same-body

  The resolution control saw 1.95x for a deliberate doubling of work, so this instrument resolves a 2x.
  The scale control — the same body under two names — came out 0.2% from 1.00.
  THE RULE FOR THE WHOLE TABLE: no ratio within 0.2% of 1.00 is a reading.

  AND IT BITES HARDER HERE THAN ON A PRICE TABLE. A price of 34x survives a 6% noise floor without
  a scratch — nobody rereads a 34x because the instrument wobbles by a twentieth of it. A decision
  that wins by 4% does not survive it at all: the margin IS the instrument. Such a row is not a
  narrow win and it is not a tie either — the decision is UNSETTLED, which is a different sentence
  from "the two designs are equal". Equal is a claim about the designs; unsettled is a statement
  about this machine at this moment, and a quieter one may separate them. Those rows print no
  margin, because a margin the instrument could not resolve is what gets quoted on.

HOW TO READ A ROW HERE — THE OPPOSITE OF THE PRICE TABLE

  ratio = ours / floor, where the floor is THE DESIGN WE REJECTED. So the direction is inverted
  against every other table in this suite:

    below 1.00   the shipped design wins. The margin column says by how much: it is 1 / ratio,
                 because 0.40x is the ratio and "2.5x cheaper" is what a person means by it.
    above 1.00   THE REJECTED DESIGN WON AND THE DECISION WAS WRONG. Marked `!`, and it is a
                 finding rather than an error in the harness. Several pairs in this catalogue were
                 written by their authors EXPECTING to lose — a delivery list against a scan at
                 50% dirty, coalescing at one write per node per frame, a field filter at one in
                 four skipped — because a comparison that only ever vindicates its author is a
                 comparison nobody should believe. Those rows are the most valuable in the suite.

WHERE THE TWO SIDES ARE NOT DOING THE SAME WORK

On a price table an unequal floor flatters us and that is the whole risk: a primitive doing less
than our call makes the abstraction look cheaper than it is. HERE THE RISK IS A DIFFERENT AND WORSE
ONE. A floor that is not the rejected design faithfully built means we may have refused a design we
never actually measured — and then printed a number saying we were right to. That is the failure this
catalogue exists to prevent, and it cannot be checked from a ratio.

So it is checked from the caveats, which are printed in full under every row below. Each one names
what the rejected design would have BOUGHT — the reason somebody proposed it — before it names
anything else, and states which way each inequality pushes that row's ratio. No claim is summarised
here that a row does not make for itself.

ONE THING IS STRUCTURAL AND APPLIES WHEREVER BOTH ARMS RUN THE ENGINE: the price tables put our code
against a `Map` that nothing of ours can spoil, and that is no longer true when both sides call the
same compiled function through the same inline caches. One side's shapes then reach the other's
measurement inside one process. The rows where that applies say so, and say which way it leans.

THE CONTROLS, IN FULL

control-resolution-2x   1.95x
  ours   two units of arithmetic per iteration
  floor  one unit of arithmetic per iteration
  NOT A PRICE — an instrument check, and it is printed first because nothing below it means
  anything until it passes. The true ratio is a little UNDER 2: both sides pay the driving
  loop once, so the exact expectation is (2u + L) / (u + L) with L the loop and u one unit,
  which at sixteen dependent steps per unit puts it around 1.9. A reading below 1.5 means this
  process could not resolve a deliberate doubling, and the runner voids the report rather than
  printing prices under it.

control-scale-same-body   1.00x
  ours   one unit of arithmetic per iteration
  floor  the same body again, over its own copy of the same state
  NOT A PRICE — the noise floor. The two sides are identical code over identical,
  separately-allocated state, so the true ratio is 1.00 exactly and every departure from it
  printed here was produced by this machine at this moment. The rule that follows is the one
  the whole table is read under: NO RATIO CLOSER TO 1.00 THAN THIS GAP IS A READING. It also
  bounds the per-row noise figure, which is the same idea measured again inside every pair —
  each pair times its floor twice, once before our side and once after, and prints how far the
  two disagreed.
