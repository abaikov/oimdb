WHERE THE FLOOR AND OUR CALL ARE NOT DOING THE SAME WORK

There is no pair in this suite whose two sides do identical work, and a floor that does less is a
lie in our favour. Two of these are structural and apply to most rows: a Map-keyed floor pays a
hash where our identity path does not, which flatters us; and our write additionally marks a channel
and may route to indexes the floor knows nothing about, which does not. Each row says its own.

A THIRD ONE BELONGS TO THE INSTRUMENT AND APPLIES TO EVERY ROW. Both sides are driven by a loop —
an increment, a bounds check, an index wrap and one store into a sink — and that loop is charged
to the numerator and the denominator alike. Adding the same constant to both pulls a ratio toward
1.00, and it pulls hardest where the operation is cheapest, so the sub-nanosecond rows are the ones
this understates most. The wrap is a mask rather than a division for exactly that reason. Read
`find.vs-map-get` as the calibration: it is one call and one Map.get on our side against one Map.get
on the floor, so whatever it reports above 1.00 is mostly this.

updateByKey.column-vs-map-get-and-store   [per write]   1.82x
  ours   collection.updateEntityColumn(collection.getSlotByKey(id), 'title', value)
  floor  const row = map.get(id); if (row !== undefined) row.title = value
  The column door by key: no patch object and no walk over one. Both sides pay the lookup;
  ours then compares the value, writes it in place and marks the entity changed once per
  frame.
  measured  1,459,382 ops/pass ours, 1,875,584 floor; 9 samples; warm-up 19 planned, 19/19 reached; spread 0.8%/3.0%; load 2.11 → 2.11 on 8 cores

remove.vs-map-delete   [per write]   1.27x
  ours   collection.clearContent(slot) — through the identity, no index registered
  floor  map.delete(id)
  THE HASH IS ON THE FLOOR'S SIDE ONLY: ours is handed the identity and the floor hashes a
  string, which flatters us — the deleteEntityByKey path is not priced here and would cost us
  that lookup too. Against that, ours clears the value through the writer, reports it on the
  channel, recomputes emptiness and asks whether anything holds or references the identity
  before it unfiles it from the store — an unheld, emptied slot is released at the call. The
  flush that closes the batch is inside the timed body. The floor's row is gone the instant
  delete returns and nothing can be subscribed to it. Both sides rebuild their population
  between passes, untimed.
  measured  8,192 ops/pass ours, 8,192 floor; 15 samples; warm-up 2000 planned, 2000/2000 reached; spread 15.0%/28.1%; load 2.02 → 2.61 on 8 cores

find.vs-map-get   [per read]   1.09x
  ours   collection.findEntityByKey(id) — the entity of a key, or undefined
  floor  map.get(id)
  THE CALIBRATION ROW. `findEntityByKey` is `findSlotByKey` — one call into the slot store
  service and one `Map.get` — and one `.entity` load off the slot, so if this reads much above
  1 the instrument is measuring call overhead rather than the library, and every other ratio
  in this table should be read with that in mind. Every key hits — a miss is a different path
  on both sides and would mix two prices.
  measured  1,957,662 ops/pass ours, 2,097,152 floor; 9 samples; warm-up 22 planned, 22/22 reached; spread 2.1%/2.7%; load 2.61 → 2.88 on 8 cores

memberCount.vs-set-size   [per read]   1.00x
  ours   bucketCollection.getBucketMemberSlotCount(bucket)
  floor  set.size
  Ours is two calls deep — the bucket collection hands the question to its bucket slots
  service, whose `size` reads the same `Set.size` — and those two calls are the whole
  difference. The floor reads a field of a container whose correctness is somebody else's
  problem. Both sides cycle through all sixty-four buckets rather than asking the same one
  repeatedly. THIS ROW ONCE READ BELOW 1.00 AND THAT NUMBER WAS A HASH, NOT A COUNT. The floor
  reached its `Set` through `sets.get(name)` — a string-keyed `Map.get` — while our side was
  handed the bucket out of an array. So the floor's number was very nearly all addressing and
  ours was very nearly all service hop, and the row reported the difference between a hash and
  an array load under the name of a member count. The floor now holds its sets in an array
  indexed exactly the way our side indexes its buckets, so both sides are addressed alike and
  what is left in the ratio is the service hop. This suite's standing rule is that a floor
  doing less is a lie in our favour; that rule was pointed at us here, and the reading it
  produced is withdrawn rather than qualified. THE CALL WAS `db.memberCount(bucket)` AND IS
  `bucketCollection.getBucketMemberSlotCount(bucket)`, on the bucket collection rather than on
  the database — the same question through a different object. Nothing recorded before
  2026-08-29 is re-attributed to it, and the prediction the old caveat carried (roughly 3.5 to
  4.4, from figures in `measurements/2026-08-20-price/read-2`) was written for the old call
  and is not a prediction about this one.
  measured  48,323,346 ops/pass ours, 49,445,600 floor; 9 samples; warm-up 20 planned, 20/20 reached; spread 1.3%/1.3%; load 2.88 → 2.88 on 8 cores

subscribe.by-key.deliver-vs-changed-ids-at-flush   [per frame]   0.92x
  ours   collection.updateEntityByKey(id, patch), flush, and the subscription fires — per key delivered
  floor  map.get(id) and store the field, add the id to a Set of changed ids, and at flush call every handler a Map of id → handlers holds for each changed id
  Both sides address the entity by its string key on every write and deliver once per flush.
  Ours resolves the key to the slot and then reaches the subscribers through `slot.effects`;
  the floor resolves the key twice, once to the row and once at flush to its handlers. One
  write per key per frame, a thousand keys, one subscriber each. The floor holds only for a
  one-column write that nothing but the subscriber listens to.
  measured  703,378 ops/pass ours, 632,812 floor; 9 samples; warm-up 24 planned, 24/24 reached; spread 90.8%/5.7%; load 2.59 → 2.59 on 8 cores

THE CONTROLS, IN FULL

control-resolution-2x   1.94x
  ours   two units of arithmetic per iteration
  floor  one unit of arithmetic per iteration
  NOT A PRICE — an instrument check, and it is printed first because nothing below it means
  anything until it passes. The true ratio is a little UNDER 2: both sides pay the driving
  loop once, so the exact expectation is (2u + L) / (u + L) with L the loop and u one unit,
  which at sixteen dependent steps per unit puts it around 1.9. A reading below 1.5 means this
  process could not resolve a deliberate doubling, and the runner voids the report rather than
  printing prices under it.

control-scale-same-body   1.00x
  ours   one unit of arithmetic per iteration
  floor  the same body again, over its own copy of the same state
  NOT A PRICE — the noise floor. The two sides are identical code over identical,
  separately-allocated state, so the true ratio is 1.00 exactly and every departure from it
  printed here was produced by this machine at this moment. The rule that follows is the one
  the whole table is read under: NO RATIO CLOSER TO 1.00 THAN THIS GAP IS A READING. It also
  bounds the per-row noise figure, which is the same idea measured again inside every pair —
  each pair times its floor twice, once before our side and once after, and prints how far the
  two disagreed.
