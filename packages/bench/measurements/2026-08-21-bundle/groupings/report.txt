OIMDB — WHAT THE SHAPE OF THE GROUPING LAYER COSTS SOMEBODY ELSE'S BUNDLE

One program, bundled five times, tree-shaken, minified and gzipped. Two of the five are
baselines — a consumer who never groups anything, and a consumer who takes one grouping and
reads it — and the other three are that same consumer under a design that was really considered
and turned down: the four groupings as METHODS on the collection class, the four modules as ONE
module over one shared memo, and the bucket mutator as an `order?` OPTION the callee resolves.
Each difference is one decision and nothing else. A single bundle size settles nothing; only the
gaps do.

  THE DECISION THIS ROW PRICES IS `packaging.a-grouping-is-a-free-function`. Every consumer of
  this library holds a POPULATION; a bundler cannot drop a class's methods one at a time; so
  under the method form the arm on the left would have carried everything the arm on the right
  carries — the four grouping bodies and every address store, membership source and mutator
  they name — without a line of the application being able to reach any of it. WHAT THE ARM ON
  THE RIGHT STANDS IN FOR: the four names are pushed rather than written as four method bodies
  inside `OIMCollection`, which loses the method form four function signatures and four module
  wrappers — tens of bytes in ITS favour, against a closure both forms carry identically. The
  decomposition below names that closure module by module, so the claim can be checked rather
  than believed. PREDICTED ≈14.8 KB minified, by adding up module sizes in the ordered-list
  artifact — arithmetic over an artifact, not a measurement, stated here so this run either
  confirms or refutes it.

  THE DECISION THIS ROW PRICES IS
  `packaging.each-grouping-is-its-own-module-with-its-own-memo`. A module exporting four
  INDEPENDENT functions would shake per export and this row would read near zero — that is not
  the design that was turned down. The one turned down is a single module over ONE memo keyed
  by the columns and a token saying which grouping it was, so one mint-and-memo helper serves
  all four shapes, and a helper branching over a token names every address store and every
  membership source from one function body. A bundler cannot see past a runtime test. WHAT THE
  ARM ON THE RIGHT STANDS IN FOR, and the direction it errs: the other three groupings are
  pushed rather than routed through a shared helper, so the shared form would write the mint
  scaffolding once instead of four times and save whatever that scaffolding costs while still
  dragging every strategy — this row is an UPPER BOUND, and the strategies are the kilobytes.
  THE REACHABILITY HALF IS NOT MEASURED HERE AND IS NOT OPEN:
  `packages/core/tests/what-an-import-retains.test.ts` already asserts, with no toolchain,
  that `groupBy` retains none of `OIMBucketAddressStoreByIdentityChain`,
  `OIMMembershipSourceDerivedMany` or `OIMMembershipSourceAll`. PREDICTED ≈4.5 KB minified for

The "sort?" column is the bundler's own answer to the question
packages/core/tests/what-an-import-retains.test.ts answers by reading import statements with no
toolchain at all. Two instruments, one fact. If they ever disagree, one of them is wrong and
finding out which is worth more than either number.

THE BREAKDOWN UNDER EACH ARM IS WHY THE COLUMN CAN BE TRUSTED TO DISAGREE. A count alone has one
way to be wrong and no way to show it; a list of modules with the bytes each one contributed can
be opened, added up, and checked against the source. database-only is printed in full because it
is the toll no consumer can avoid — what this engine charges an application that constructs a
database and asks for nothing else, module by module. The longer arms are cut to the largest
few, with the count and the bytes of the remainder on the line below; records.json keeps every
module of every arm.

IF ANY OF THESE THREE COMES OUT SMALL

Then the prediction beside it was wrong and the prediction is what should go, not the row. All
three were arrived at by adding up module sizes in the ordered-list artifact rather than by
building anything, which is exactly the move this repository calls arithmetic over an artifact
and refuses to publish. This run is the first time any of them meets a bundler. A refuted
prediction here costs nothing, because none of the three has ever been cited anywhere.

WHAT A SMALL NUMBER WOULD NOT DO IS OVERTURN ANY OF THE THREE DECISIONS. Each has two halves and
only one of them is a size. The other is reachability, and it is not a matter of degree: a
method is in the bundle of everyone who holds the class, a shared memo is in the bundle of
everyone who takes one grouping, and a ternary over an option is in the bundle of everyone who
groups at all. `packages/core/tests/what-an-import-retains.test.ts` pins that half already, with
no toolchain and no threshold, and it goes on holding at 14 KB, at 1 KB and at forty bytes.

WHAT WOULD DESERVE ATTENTION IS THE ORDER OF THE THREE. The layer row is measured against a
consumer who never groups and the other two against a consumer who groups once, so they are not
competing for the same reader — but if the shared module or the order option turned out to be
the larger of the two, that would say where the weight of this layer actually sits, and it would
say it about the caller this library is mostly for: somebody who groups one column and sorts
nothing.

AND IF THE LAYER ROW IS THE LARGEST BY FAR, that is not a licence to quote it as what the
groupings "cost". It is what a design nobody shipped would have cost a consumer who never
groups. The shipped design charges that consumer nothing at all, which is the arm on the left,
and its own number is in the table below.
