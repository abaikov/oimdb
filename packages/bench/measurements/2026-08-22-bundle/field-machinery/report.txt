OIMDB — WHAT THE FIELD MACHINERY COSTS AN APPLICATION THAT NEVER TOUCHES IT

ONE application, bundled twice, tree-shaken, minified and gzipped. It files rows into a
population, keeps one index whose membership it writes by hand, subscribes and reads — and it
declares no `dependsOn` and calls no `subscribeToField`, so the field-recording and
column-routing apparatus is inert for it from the first line to the last. That half is already
settled without a bundler: a manual membership source declares `selects?: undefined`, so
`dependents` stays empty and the routing loop returns on `size === 0`, and every recording site
is behind `slot.movedFields !== undefined`, which nothing sets until a field subscription
arrives. ZERO TIME, AND THE BYTES ARE THE OPEN QUESTION. THE TWO ARMS DIFFER IN THE ENGINE, NOT
IN THE CONSUMER, and they have to: the grouping split already happened, so a consumer can choose
to import from `src/groupings/` and two real programs exist — nothing a consumer writes can
decline `recordMovedFields`, so two arms over today's engine would differ by exactly zero. The
second arm is therefore a COUNTERFACTUAL BUILD: the same consumer module, against
`packages/core/src` with the apparatus removed in memory, member by member, every rule asserted
by occurrence count and every one of them printed under the arm below.

The "record?" column is the bundler's own answer to the question
packages/core/tests/what-an-import-retains.test.ts answers by reading import statements with no
toolchain at all. Two instruments, one fact. If they ever disagree, one of them is wrong and
finding out which is worth more than either number.

THE BREAKDOWN UNDER EACH ARM IS WHY THE COLUMN CAN BE TRUSTED TO DISAGREE. A count alone has one
way to be wrong and no way to show it; a list of modules with the bytes each one contributed can
be opened, added up, and checked against the source. database-only is printed in full because it
is the toll no consumer can avoid — what this engine charges an application that constructs a
database and asks for nothing else, module by module. The longer arms are cut to the largest
few, with the count and the bytes of the remainder on the line below; records.json keeps every
module of every arm.

IF THIS COMES OUT SMALL — AND IT MAY WELL

THEN THE QUESTION IS CLOSED, AND THAT IS THE RESULT. `recordMovedFields.ts` is a small module
and the routing is a short loop over a map that is empty. A measured "this is not worth
splitting" is worth more than a split nobody needed, and it is worth more than the open question
the header of `OIMCollection.ts` leaves behind: that file records the argument for moving eight
strategies to `src/groupings/`, the field machinery was not moved with them, and no entry
anywhere says why. A few hundred bytes here is that entry.

IT WOULD NOT MEAN THE APPARATUS IS FREE. This row is a LOWER BOUND and every omission in the
excision points the same way — the imports are left in, the membership source keeps its empty
`assertColumnsAreDeclared`, `OIMSingletonEntitySlot` is untouched, and every doc comment of
every removed member stays. A real split would take more out than this arm did, so the true
saving is at least this number rather than at most it.

AND A LARGE NUMBER WOULD NOT BE A DESIGN ON ITS OWN. What this arm does is DELETE; what a split
has to do is MOVE, and this does not move as cleanly as four grouping functions did.
`reindexDependents` is on the write path of every population, so lifting it means a caller who
declares a column installs something that path then calls — an indirection on the hottest path
this engine has, which is a cost in nanoseconds against a saving in bytes. This piece prices one
side of that trade and says nothing about the other; `packages/bench/src/price/` is where the
other side would be measured, and it has not been.

WHAT DESERVES ATTENTION EITHER WAY IS THE SHAPE OF THE BREAKDOWN. If the difference is mostly
`core/recordMovedFields.ts` and `core/OIMDB.ts`, this is a database-side surface and the
population barely pays for it — which would mean the header of `OIMCollection.ts` names the
wrong owner for the argument. If it is mostly `abstract/OIMCollection.ts`, that header names the
right one, and the machinery's omission from the grouping move is the thing that wants
explaining.
