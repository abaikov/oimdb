WHERE THE FLOOR AND OUR CALL ARE NOT DOING THE SAME WORK

There is no pair in this suite whose two sides do identical work, and a floor that does less is a
lie in our favour. Two of these are structural and apply to most rows: a Map-keyed floor pays a
hash where our identity path does not, which flatters us; and our write additionally marks a channel
and may route to indexes the floor knows nothing about, which does not. Each row says its own.

A THIRD ONE BELONGS TO THE INSTRUMENT AND APPLIES TO EVERY ROW. Both sides are driven by a loop —
an increment, a bounds check, an index wrap and one store into a sink — and that loop is charged
to the numerator and the denominator alike. Adding the same constant to both pulls a ratio toward
1.00, and it pulls hardest where the operation is cheapest, so the sub-nanosecond rows are the ones
this understates most. The wrap is a mask rather than a division for exactly that reason. Read
`find.vs-map-get` as the calibration: it is one call and one Map.get on our side against one Map.get
on the floor, so whatever it reports above 1.00 is mostly this.

sweep.grouped-column.manual-set-members   [per write]   180x
  ours   entityService.slotContentService.updateEntity(slot, { group }), slotContentService.writeBucketMemberSlots on both buckets, then slotContentService.getBucketMemberSlots(bucket)
  floor  move the row between two Sets, write the column, iterate one Set
  THE ARM IS THE WHOLE-BUCKET DOOR, IN PLACE. THE MEMBERSHIP IS THE CALLER'S ON THIS ARM: the
  bucket collection is not an index and hears no write, so the move is made in the same
  statement sequence as the write, inside the timed body. WHAT THE ARM PAYS THAT THE FLOOR
  DOES NOT: `updateEntity` walks the patch, gates the column, builds the moved-column list and
  marks the row on the channel; then the move marks both buckets and keeps the slot's hold
  count. THE ADDRESS IS RESOLVED THE SAME WAY ON BOTH SIDES: one `getSlotByKey` per end of the
  move, which is the hash the floor pays as `byGroup.get(next)`. THE OLD GROUP IS READ OFF THE
  STORED ROW BEFORE THE WRITE LANDS, which a caller holding the slot can always do.
  `writeBucketMemberSlots` is handed each bucket's new membership, asks the bucket content
  service whether the bucket already holds exactly those slots, and — since it does not —
  replaces the set and reports who arrived and who left. Moving one row is therefore TWO FULL
  BUCKET REWRITES, plus the caller's own `indexOf` and `splice` on the array it keeps beside
  them, on groups of a hundred and twenty-eight. THIS IS THE DOOR A RE-HYDRATED SERVER LIST
  USES and pricing it on a one-row move is pricing it on its worst task; the distance between
  it and the other arms is what the finer doors are worth. THE FLOOR IS THE SAME CODE FOR
  EVERY ARM OF THIS TASK, which is the property that makes the arms comparable at all. It
  moves the row between two `Set`s — the container our unordered bucket holds its members in,
  so this is the structural equal and not a cheaper shape — writes the column, and iterates
  one group. WHAT IT DOES LESS OF, and every item pushes our ratio UP: it keeps no identity
  that outlives the value, so nothing can be subscribed to a row or to a group; it stamps no
  clock; it records no moved columns; it keeps no hold count, so a group cannot be resolved by
  address and handed to a consumer; and its groups can be read by iterating them and by
  nothing else. WHAT IS ON BOTH SIDES AND CANCELS: the read group is held from outside the
  clock on both, and the read visits the same members on both. WHERE THE FLOOR IS CHARGED MORE
  THAN IT SHOULD BE, stated because it flatters us: it hashes `row.group` to find the
  departure set, where the index arm reads the previous key off the slot's own index-key cell.
  measured  2,048 ops/pass ours, 537,188 floor; 9 samples; warm-up 24 planned, 24/24 reached; spread 5.2%/89.3%; load 1.54 → 1.54 on 8 cores

upsert.whole-row-vs-five-stores   [per write]   32.8x
  ours   slotContentService.upsertEntity(entityStoreSlot, row) — a key that already carries a value
  floor  copy five columns onto the stored object, one store each
  THE FLOOR CANNOT EXPRESS ONE HALF OF WHAT UPSERT PROMISES. An upsert of an existing key also
  REMOVES the columns the new value does not carry, which means walking the stored row to find
  them; here both rows carry all five columns, so that walk finds nothing and the floor's five
  stores are the same net effect. Ours additionally gates each column on the default equality,
  so only the column that actually moved is written and reported, where the floor writes all
  five every time. THAT DEFAULT IS SAMEVALUEZERO AND WAS AN INLINE `===` WHEN THIS ROW WAS
  RECORDED: the four columns this fixture does not move settle on the first clause and cost
  what they cost before, and the one that moves carries one comparison this figure never saw.
  Direction: mixed — the gate saves us four stores and costs us five comparisons plus a
  moved-list allocation, and the marking and routing are ours alone. Read it beside
  update.column: the difference between the two is what sending a whole row costs over sending
  the column that changed. THE STRUCTURAL CAVEAT AT THE HEAD OF THIS REPORT RUNS BACKWARDS ON
  THIS ROW AND ON NO OTHER WRITE ROW. It says a Map-keyed floor pays a hash our identity path
  does not, which flatters us. Here it is the reverse: the floor is handed `held[i]` by its
  index and never looks anything up, while ours is handed a ROW and must resolve its key —
  `keyOf` and then the store lookup inside `writeContentByKey`, a `Map.get` over eight
  thousand string keys. That lookup is inside our number and outside the floor's, so this row
  overstates what the design costs a caller who already holds the identity.
  `upsert.whole-row-vs-map-get-and-five-stores` is the same measurement with the lookup on
  both sides; the difference between the two rows is the hash, and neither alone is the price
  of an upsert.
  measured  425,900 ops/pass ours, 13,725,414 floor; 9 samples; warm-up 20 planned, 20/20 reached; spread 42.9%/0.6%; load 4.00 → 4.01 on 8 cores

graph.dependencies.inactive-branch.s3   [per write]   21.2x
  ours   updateEntityColumn on the unread branch value, then db.flush(); the effect declares flag, active and inactive as sources
  floor  inactive(v) with no batch; the alien effect reads flag() and then active() only
  ONE OPERATION IS ONE WRITE TO THE INACTIVE BRANCH AND ONE SETTLE. An effect reads a flag and
  then one of two values, and the flag selects the first, so the written value is one the body
  does not read. ARCHITECTURAL POINT: declared against auto-tracked dependencies, measured on
  a conditional branch. Ours has no auto-tracking: the flag and both values are declared as
  the effect's sources, so a write to the unread value schedules the effect and the flush runs
  the body. Alien-signals subscribed the effect only to what its last run read, so the write
  finds no subscriber and nothing runs; the floor is a signal store with no one listening.
  EXPECTED BODY RUNS PER OPERATION [FIGURE STATED IN ADVANCE]: alien 0, ours 1; both run
  counts are sunk into priceSink.number at the end of each pass. The ratio is therefore large
  by construction and prices the work ours does that the floor skips, not a slower path doing
  the same work. Ours is @oimdb/core from source: a void-key entity store slot root
  (createStoreSlot, slotContentService.upsertEntity, holdSingleSlot) of an
  OIMSingleSlotService from createOIMSingleEntityService, written through
  slotContentService.updateEntityColumn, void-key computed store slots
  (createComputedStoreSlot, computedCollectionService.slotContentService.getValueForSlot) of
  an OIMComputedService for anything derived, one effect from OIMEffectService, and db.flush()
  as the settle. The floor is alien-signals 3.2: signal, computed, effect, and
  startBatch/endBatch where the row says so. Every write stores a new number from a counter,
  so no write on either arm is stopped by a same-value check. Each arm runs in its own

subscribeToField.deliver-vs-filtered-queue-at-flush   [per frame]   14.3x
  ours   effectService.createEffect([column slot of 'title'], [], h) — the narrowest signal the engine has
  floor  store the field, queue the entity once when the moved name is 'title', and call h for each queued entity at flush
  The one column that moved is the one subscribed to, on both sides, and both deliver once per
  flush after every write of the frame has landed. A column of an entity is its own identity
  in ours, minted by `holdColumn`, and a write marks only the column slots that exist, so a
  `note` write does not reach a `title` subscriber at all. The mint is outside the clock (one
  per entity, at build); inside it ours pays the write path, the column mark, the enlist and

  NOT A PRICE — an instrument check, and it is printed first because nothing below it means
  anything until it passes. The true ratio is a little UNDER 2: both sides pay the driving
