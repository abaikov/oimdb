OIMDB — THE PRICE OF EVERY OPERATION AGAINST THE PRIMITIVE IT REPLACES

Each row is one call this library offers, timed against the plain JavaScript a person would have
written instead of it. Not against another library — against the floor. The ratio is ours over
the floor, and it is a price tag rather than a ranking.

WHERE THE FLOOR AND OUR CALL ARE NOT DOING THE SAME WORK

There is no pair in this suite whose two sides do identical work, and a floor that does less is a
lie in our favour. Two of these are structural and apply to most rows: a Map-keyed floor pays a
hash where our identity path does not, which flatters us; and our write additionally marks a channel
and may route to indexes the floor knows nothing about, which does not. Each row says its own.

A THIRD ONE BELONGS TO THE INSTRUMENT AND APPLIES TO EVERY ROW. Both sides are driven by a loop —
an increment, a bounds check, an index wrap and one store into a sink — and that loop is charged
to the numerator and the denominator alike. Adding the same constant to both pulls a ratio toward
1.00, and it pulls hardest where the operation is cheapest, so the sub-nanosecond rows are the ones
this understates most. The wrap is a mask rather than a division for exactly that reason. Read
`find.vs-map-get` as the calibration: it is one call and one Map.get on our side against one Map.get
on the floor, so whatever it reports above 1.00 is mostly this.

  floor  store the field and call the handler — per row

update.indexed-column-vs-hand-index   [per write]   5.58x
  ours   collection.updateEntity(slot, { group }) — an OIMIndex over `group` re-files the row
  floor  delete from the old Set, store the column, add to the new Set
  THE INDEX IS AUTOMATIC AGAIN, SO THIS IS THE ORIGINAL QUESTION. `OIMIndex` hears its source
  collection through a content-change listener: `updateEntity` marks the row, the listener
  reads the key the row was last filed under from the row's own `indexKeys` cell, compares it
  with `keyOf(entity)` and moves the slot with `removeBucketMemberSlotByKey` +
  `addBucketMemberSlotByKey`. The caller writes one column and nothing else. All sixty-four
  buckets are held before the clock, because an index files only into buckets that exist. A
  reading recorded under this id while the move was the caller's (`removeSlotAt` +
  `appendSlot` on a positional bucket collection) priced a different body and must not be
  quoted against this one. WHERE THE TWO SIDES ARE NOT DOING THE SAME WORK. Ours resolves BOTH
  buckets by key through the bucket store (two `Map.get`), as the floor resolves both `Set`s;
  the unordered bucket is a `Set`, so leave and join are hashes on both sides. Ours
  additionally reads and writes the row's index cell, holds and unholds the row from the
  buckets, queues both buckets for the change-close flush that marks them on the channel so a
  subscriber can be woken, and marks the row itself — and the flush that closes the batch is
  inside the timed body. The floor can be read by iterating it and by nothing else. Direction:
  inflates our ratio.

hold.vs-map-get   [per relationship]   1.24x
  ours   collection.holdSlotByKey(id) — resolve a key to an identity and keep it, minting on a miss
  floor  map.get(id)
  THE FLOOR CANNOT MINT AND CANNOT KEEP, and those are the two differences. `holdSlotByKey`
  promises an identity for a key the population has never seen, so a caller can subscribe to a
  row before it arrives; and the hold is a COUNTED REFERENCE, so nothing can unfile that
  identity while the caller still has it. Every key here HITS, so the minting branch never
  runs — what is left over the map read is the store call, the `=== undefined` compare that is
  false on every hit, and one `holdCount++` (`OIMSlotAggregate.holdSlotByKey` into
  `OIMSlotOwner.holdSlot`). THE TWO ALLOCATIONS THIS CAVEAT CHARGED NO LONGER EXIST AND
  NOTHING REPLACES THEM. It read "TWO ALLOCATIONS PER CALL: the `{ slot, release }` record and
  the release closure `holdSlot` builds", and that was the charge the row was built to expose:
  a minting door that allocated where a `Map.get` does not. No door hands back such a record —
  `holdSlotByKey` returns the slot and increments one field — so the hit path allocates
  nothing on either side and what is left between the two columns is one call and one store.
  Direction: inflates our ratio, now by the store call and the increment alone. READ IT
  AGAINST `find.vs-map-get` DIRECTLY ABOVE — the gap between the two is what the minting door

  THIS ROW EXISTS TO SPLIT `flush.empty-vs-noop`, WHICH IS A PRICE AGAINST NOTHING. That row
  puts an empty frame against a counter bump, so its multiple prices the existence of a frame
  rather than anything inside one, and it cannot say which part of the absolute figure is the

THE CONTROLS, IN FULL

control-resolution-2x   1.94x
  ours   two units of arithmetic per iteration
  floor  one unit of arithmetic per iteration
  NOT A PRICE — an instrument check, and it is printed first because nothing below it means
  anything until it passes. The true ratio is a little UNDER 2: both sides pay the driving
  loop once, so the exact expectation is (2u + L) / (u + L) with L the loop and u one unit,
  which at sixteen dependent steps per unit puts it around 1.9. A reading below 1.5 means this
  process could not resolve a deliberate doubling, and the runner voids the report rather than
  printing prices under it.

control-scale-same-body   1.01x
  ours   one unit of arithmetic per iteration
  floor  the same body again, over its own copy of the same state
  NOT A PRICE — the noise floor. The two sides are identical code over identical,
  separately-allocated state, so the true ratio is 1.00 exactly and every departure from it
  printed here was produced by this machine at this moment. The rule that follows is the one
  the whole table is read under: NO RATIO CLOSER TO 1.00 THAN THIS GAP IS A READING. It also
  bounds the per-row noise figure, which is the same idea measured again inside every pair —
  each pair times its floor twice, once before our side and once after, and prints how far the
  two disagreed.
