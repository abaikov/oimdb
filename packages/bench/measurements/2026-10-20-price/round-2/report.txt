WHERE THE FLOOR AND OUR CALL ARE NOT DOING THE SAME WORK

There is no pair in this suite whose two sides do identical work, and a floor that does less is a
lie in our favour. Two of these are structural and apply to most rows: a Map-keyed floor pays a
hash where our identity path does not, which flatters us; and our write additionally marks a channel
and may route to indexes the floor knows nothing about, which does not. Each row says its own.

A THIRD ONE BELONGS TO THE INSTRUMENT AND APPLIES TO EVERY ROW. Both sides are driven by a loop —
an increment, a bounds check, an index wrap and one store into a sink — and that loop is charged
to the numerator and the denominator alike. Adding the same constant to both pulls a ratio toward
1.00, and it pulls hardest where the operation is cheapest, so the sub-nanosecond rows are the ones
this understates most. The wrap is a mask rather than a division for exactly that reason. Read
`find.vs-map-get` as the calibration: it is one call and one Map.get on our side against one Map.get
on the floor, so whatever it reports above 1.00 is mostly this.

  THE ARM IS THE WHOLE-BUCKET DOOR, IN PLACE. THE MEMBERSHIP IS THE CALLER'S ON THIS ARM: the
  bucket collection is not an index and hears no write, so the move is made in the same
  statement sequence as the write, inside the timed body. WHAT THE ARM PAYS THAT THE FLOOR
  DOES NOT: `updateEntity` walks the patch, gates the column, builds the moved-column list and
  marks the row on the channel; then the move marks both buckets and keeps the slot's hold
  count. THE ADDRESS IS RESOLVED THE SAME WAY ON BOTH SIDES: one `getSlotByKey` per end of the
  move, which is the hash the floor pays as `byGroup.get(next)`. THE OLD GROUP IS READ OFF THE
  STORED ROW BEFORE THE WRITE LANDS, which a caller holding the slot can always do.
  `writeContent` is handed each bucket's new membership, asks the bucket service whether the
  bucket already holds exactly those slots, and — since it does not — replaces the set and
  reports who arrived and who left. Moving one row is therefore TWO FULL BUCKET REWRITES, plus
  the caller's own `indexOf` and `splice` on the array it keeps beside them, on groups of a
  hundred and twenty-eight. THIS IS THE DOOR A RE-HYDRATED SERVER LIST USES and pricing it on
  a one-row move is pricing it on its worst task; the distance between it and the other arms
  is what the finer doors are worth. THE FLOOR IS THE SAME CODE FOR EVERY ARM OF THIS TASK,
  which is the property that makes the arms comparable at all. It moves the row between two
  `Set`s — the container our unordered bucket holds its members in, so this is the structural
  equal and not a cheaper shape — writes the column, and iterates one group. WHAT IT DOES LESS
  OF, and every item pushes our ratio UP: it keeps no identity that outlives the value, so
  nothing can be subscribed to a row or to a group; it stamps no clock; it records no moved
  columns; it keeps no hold count, so a group cannot be resolved by address and handed to a
  consumer; and its groups can be read by iterating them and by nothing else. WHAT IS ON BOTH
  SIDES AND CANCELS: the read group is held from outside the clock on both, and the read
  visits the same members on both. WHERE THE FLOOR IS CHARGED MORE THAN IT SHOULD BE, stated
  because it flatters us: it hashes `row.group` to find the departure set, where the index arm

THE CONTROLS, IN FULL

control-resolution-2x   1.93x
  ours   two units of arithmetic per iteration
  floor  one unit of arithmetic per iteration
  NOT A PRICE — an instrument check, and it is printed first because nothing below it means
  anything until it passes. The true ratio is a little UNDER 2: both sides pay the driving
  loop once, so the exact expectation is (2u + L) / (u + L) with L the loop and u one unit,
  which at sixteen dependent steps per unit puts it around 1.9. A reading below 1.5 means this
  process could not resolve a deliberate doubling, and the runner voids the report rather than
  printing prices under it.

control-scale-same-body   1.02x
  ours   one unit of arithmetic per iteration
  floor  the same body again, over its own copy of the same state
  NOT A PRICE — the noise floor. The two sides are identical code over identical,
  separately-allocated state, so the true ratio is 1.00 exactly and every departure from it
  printed here was produced by this machine at this moment. The rule that follows is the one
  the whole table is read under: NO RATIO CLOSER TO 1.00 THAN THIS GAP IS A READING. It also
  bounds the per-row noise figure, which is the same idea measured again inside every pair —
  each pair times its floor twice, once before our side and once after, and prints how far the
  two disagreed.
