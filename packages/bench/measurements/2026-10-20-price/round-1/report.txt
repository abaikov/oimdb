WHERE THE FLOOR AND OUR CALL ARE NOT DOING THE SAME WORK

There is no pair in this suite whose two sides do identical work, and a floor that does less is a
lie in our favour. Two of these are structural and apply to most rows: a Map-keyed floor pays a
hash where our identity path does not, which flatters us; and our write additionally marks a channel
and may route to indexes the floor knows nothing about, which does not. Each row says its own.

A THIRD ONE BELONGS TO THE INSTRUMENT AND APPLIES TO EVERY ROW. Both sides are driven by a loop —
an increment, a bounds check, an index wrap and one store into a sink — and that loop is charged
to the numerator and the denominator alike. Adding the same constant to both pulls a ratio toward
1.00, and it pulls hardest where the operation is cheapest, so the sub-nanosecond rows are the ones
this understates most. The wrap is a mask rather than a division for exactly that reason. Read
`find.vs-map-get` as the calibration: it is one call and one Map.get on our side against one Map.get
on the floor, so whatever it reports above 1.00 is mostly this.

computed.recompute-vs-hand-memo   [per frame]   13.2x
  ours   a held computed slot over one entity, refreshed by its OIMComputedCollection at the read after the frame
  floor  set a dirty flag on write, recompute on read if dirty
  THE FLOOR IS A REAL MEMO AND DOES THE SAME JOB: it is invalidated by the write, it
  recomputes once however many writes landed, and it answers the same number. ONE HALF OF THIS
  CAVEAT WAS TRUE OF THE OLD ENGINE AND IS NOT TRUE OF THIS ONE. It said the floor recomputes
  LAZILY at the read where ours recomputes EAGERLY during the frame, so a value nobody reads
  was free for the floor and paid for by us. A derivation is a slot that nothing ever marks
  now — it is asked, not woken — so both sides are lazy and a value nobody reads is free on
  both. WHAT THAT LEAVES IN THE RATIO is the settle itself: ours compares the clock stamp of
  every source against the stamp it last derived at and only then runs the body, where the
  floor reads one boolean. WHAT OURS STILL BUYS AND THE FLOOR CANNOT: the graph. A memo over
  another memo has to be invalidated by hand and in the right order; `oimRefreshComputedSlot`
  refreshes its source computeds first. The 1024 derivations are the slots of ONE
  `OIMComputedCollection` keyed by position, each held, so the read is `getValueForSlot` on an
  identity resolved before the clock and no key is looked up. That is not exercised by the
  one-level derivation here, so it is charged to us and never credited — direction: inflates
  our ratio, by an amount a deeper graph would show and this row cannot. Ours additionally
  gates on the comparator before marking anything downstream, and the derivation here always
  changes, so that gate never saves us a wake-up.
  measured  889,770 ops/pass ours, 6,111,177 floor; 9 samples; warm-up 26 planned, 26/26 reached; spread 15.1%/7.5%; load 3.21 → 3.21 on 8 cores

index.hold-every-group-of-a-loaded-population-vs-hand-built-map   [per declaration]   2.28x
  ours   createOIMIndexSetDriven over `group` on a population already holding a thousand rows, holdSlotsByKeys every group, then dispose and unholdSlot
  floor  build a Map of Sets from the same thousand rows, then clear it
  THE ENGINE WALKS THE POPULATION AGAIN, SO THIS IS A DECLARATION ONCE MORE. It replaces
  `index-fill.over-a-loaded-population-vs-hand-built-map`, whose caller did the walk and the
  per-bucket write because the index of that day touched no population; the auto-index files
  every row it can see into a bucket the moment the bucket is minted
  (`OIMIndex.collectSourceSlotsForKeys`, through `collectBucketMemberSlotsForKey`), so a
  `holdSlotsByKeys` over all sixty-four groups is one walk of the thousand rows, one `keyOf`
  per row, one index-key write per row and one hold per row. No figure recorded under the old
  id attaches to this one. BOTH SIDES BUILD AND THEN GIVE BACK INSIDE THE CLOCK: ours disposes
  the index — which drops its listener, releases its index-key position (a second walk of the
  population, clearing each row's cell) and clears every bucket, unholding each row — and then
  unholds the sixty-four buckets so each is released; the floor clears every set and then the
  map. The floor reads `row.group` off held copies; ours reads it through `slot.entity`. What
  ours buys that the floor cannot: the index keeps filing every later write, and each bucket
  is an identity that can be subscribed. Direction: inflates our ratio; paid once per index.

THE CONTROLS, IN FULL

control-resolution-2x   1.94x
  ours   two units of arithmetic per iteration
  floor  one unit of arithmetic per iteration
  NOT A PRICE — an instrument check, and it is printed first because nothing below it means
  anything until it passes. The true ratio is a little UNDER 2: both sides pay the driving
  loop once, so the exact expectation is (2u + L) / (u + L) with L the loop and u one unit,
  which at sixteen dependent steps per unit puts it around 1.9. A reading below 1.5 means this
  process could not resolve a deliberate doubling, and the runner voids the report rather than
  printing prices under it.

control-scale-same-body   1.00x
  ours   one unit of arithmetic per iteration
  floor  the same body again, over its own copy of the same state
  NOT A PRICE — the noise floor. The two sides are identical code over identical,
  separately-allocated state, so the true ratio is 1.00 exactly and every departure from it
  printed here was produced by this machine at this moment. The rule that follows is the one
  the whole table is read under: NO RATIO CLOSER TO 1.00 THAN THIS GAP IS A READING. It also
  bounds the per-row noise figure, which is the same idea measured again inside every pair —
  each pair times its floor twice, once before our side and once after, and prints how far the
  two disagreed.
